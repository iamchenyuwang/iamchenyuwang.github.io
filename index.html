My research centers on one question: how can we model the cognition of human scientists—and go beyond it—to build AI agents capable of proposing novel and impactful scientific ideas? I develop agents and world models that reason over scientific knowledge, use tools and simulators, and turn hypotheses into testable designs. Research

My research asks how the core processes of scientific cognition—abstraction, analogy, hypothesis formation, experimental design, and revision from evidence—can be made computational, and how AI agents can extend those processes beyond current human workflows. My goal is not merely to automate existing research tasks, but to build systems that originate ideas that are both genuinely novel and scientifically consequential.

I pursue this question through agentic reasoning and world models: agents should form hypotheses, construct and revise models of a domain, interact with simulators and experimental tools, and learn from the resulting evidence. I use computer systems and architecture as a concrete testbed, where ideas can be evaluated against explicit physical and computational constraints.
